Private aviation in AI Search: who gets named, who gets cited, who gets overlooked

By Oliver Varcoe, MV & Co. · Published 6 October 2026

Abstract and methodology

When a wealthy buyer asks an AI assistant which private jet company to use, which firms get named? What does the assistant search for? Which sources does it trust? This is a descriptive study of how ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude answer those questions for private jet charter, brokerage, aircraft management and jet-card firms in the UK, Monaco and the French Riviera, Switzerland, Italy, Malta, the UAE, Saudi Arabia and Qatar. It tests no hypothesis and builds no score. It records what the models do.

An ongoing study

We keep re-running the questions. Each edition of this report reflects all the runs collected so far, so the figures will move as the data grows.

The questions are synthetic

Nobody outside the AI companies knows exactly what buyers type, so we wrote ten plausible questions across the buying journey. Read the results as patterns in how the models behave, not as any firm's market share.

How to read the numbers

Percentages are shares of answers. “Named in 50% of answers” means the firm was mentioned at least once in half of them; a passing mention counts. The same question rarely gets the same list twice, so read each figure as how often a firm appears, not as a ranking.

What this means

When a buyer asks an AI which jet company to use, a handful of large names are the default answer. Air Charter Service, VistaJet, NetJets and LunaJets account for 32% of all mentions of firms, and Air Charter Service appeared in 51% of answers, including those about Saudi Arabia, Geneva and the Monaco Grand Prix. All three models named all four, with and without search, so this is not one model's quirk.

For a smaller firm: these four set the bar for being 'top of mind' for an AI. Being absent from a list with room for six to ten names is the norm, not a sign that something is broken.

What this means

General questions ('best', 'top', 'rank') return the big names. Small firms surface when the question is specific: a city, a route, a style of service.

For a smaller firm: your best chance of being named is a specific question that matches what you are known for. That makes it worth being clearly associated online with your routes, your base airport and your style of service.

Finding 3

Each AI researches the question differently

Background

What a fan-out query is

When search is on, the AI does not simply look up the buyer's question. It breaks the question into several web searches of its own, written and run behind the scenes, each looking for one piece of the answer: a list of operators, a specific firm's website, a regulation, a price. One question “fans out” into many searches, which is why they are called fan-out queries. The buyer never sees them; they see only the finished answer and its links. Fan-out queries show how each model goes about researching an answer, and which firms it already had in mind and chose to check by name.

With search switched on, the three models behave like three different researchers.

What this means

Gemini reads around the subject: a handful of broad searches, then about 20 sources per answer, including marketplaces, comparison sites, Reddit and Wikipedia. ChatGPT goes to the source: about 11 narrow searches per answer, almost half of them inside a named firm's own website, and 45% of its links point to those firms. Claude barely searches: usually one generic 'best … 2026' search, and in 20% of answers none at all.

For a smaller firm: on ChatGPT, your own website is the source the model checks you against. On Gemini, what others say about you (marketplaces, guides, forums) carries more weight. Claude relies mostly on what it already knows. Across all three, a firm the model searched for by name almost always ended up in the answer (88% to 100% of the time, depending on the model), and ChatGPT searched for nearly nine in ten of the firms it went on to name: the shortlist is formed before the search, and the search checks it. On the trust questions (“broker or operator?”, “is this broker legitimate?”) no search named a firm at all, and on the boutique-broker question the searches named small firms such as SHY Aviation and Oxygen Aviation.

Finding 5

The AI reads far more than it cites

Background

How an AI with search builds an answer

When search is switched on, the model does not answer straight away. It first runs its own web searches, pulls a set of pages into its working memory and reads them, then writes the answer. This is called retrieval, or RAG (retrieval-augmented generation). The links you see under an answer are citations: the pages the model chose to credit. Many other pages were read along the way and shaped the answer, but are never shown to the user. A firm can therefore influence an answer without being cited, and be read without being credited.

Method and limits

  • Design: descriptive research. Ten synthetic buyer questions, chosen for relevance to founders of charter, broker and management firms, each asked repeatedly to each model with web search off (memory) and on (search). The study is ongoing; this edition reflects all runs collected to date, and figures will change as runs are added.
  • Models: Gemini ChatGPT Claude via each provider's API: Gemini 3.5 Flash, GPT-6 Luna and Claude Sonnet 5.5. The consumer apps add their own instructions, location and personalisation, so they can answer differently.
  • What is recorded: the full answer, every search query the model ran, every page it cited and, for ChatGPT and Claude, every page its searches returned. Google's API does not reveal the pages Gemini reads.
  • Matching firms: names in answers are matched automatically against a list of firms we track in this sector (charter, broker, management, jet-card and fractional firms with an office or base in the eight markets, plus marketplaces and others the AI is likely to name). A passing mention counts as a mention. Firms not yet on the list were identified by hand and are incomplete.
  • Limits: ten questions is a small set, and which markets and firms can appear depends on the questions asked; Italy, Malta and Qatar had no question of their own. Answers vary from run to run, so figures are appearance rates, not rankings. Source types were classified by hand.
  • Disclosure: MV&Co. works with Private Jet Media, a media business in this sector. It is not on the firm list and was not mentioned in any answer, search or citation.
